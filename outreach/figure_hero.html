What a detection-independent gate actually contains

A gate that never reads the attack: what it stops, and what walks straight through

Measured on mobius-browser-guard v0.3.1, a permission gate for Claude in Chrome. Every verdict below is the gate's own output on a real tool call — no verdict is asserted from memory.

The action ladder

The gate classifies each browser tool call by what it can do, never by whether its text looks malicious. Allow proceeds silently; Ask requires a human decision.

Allow — proceeds silently Ask — human must approve
Browser actionVerdictWhy
Read the page● Allowread-only
Navigate to an allowlisted site● Allowhost on the allowlist
Fill a form field on that site● Allowwrite into a tab of known, trusted origin
Click a control● Askactuates the page

Drop a screenshot into the attacker's page● Askwrite into a tab of untrusted origin
A batch quietly containing a click● Asknested calls judged recursively

The negative result

Against 63 attacker goals from the InjecAgent corpus, three realization patterns reach the goal without ever triggering a prompt — and between them they cover all 63. Two of the three are declared scope, not defects: reading an authenticated page is what a browser agent is for.
