A gate that never reads the attack: what it stops, and what walks straight through

Measured on mobius-browser-guard v0.3.0, a permission gate for Claude in Chrome. Every verdict below is the gate's own output on a real tool call — no verdict is asserted from memory.

The action ladder

The gate classifies each browser tool call by what it can do, never by whether its text looks malicious. Allow proceeds silently; Ask requires a human decision.

The negative result

Against 63 attacker goals from the InjecAgent corpus, three realization patterns reach the goal without ever triggering a prompt — and between them they cover all 63. Two of the three are declared scope, not defects: reading an authenticated page is what a browser agent is for.

What adversarial review found that we did not

Reviewers were instructed to refute our findings and to default to "not a hole". They rejected three of our claimed holes as declared scope — then found sharper ones. The second round reviewed the paper and found that our own fix had opened a worse hole than it closed.

The critical one: the component added in round 1 to fix a reviewer's finding learned tab origins by scanning any tool response — so a page could forge its own origin by printing one line in its body, and two "closed" channels re-opened. Fixed in v0.3.0, with a regression test.

Prompts scale with the number of new hosts visited, so a session touching many unfamiliar sites costs proportionally more. On work confined to sites you have allowlisted, the ladder is close to free.

What can honestly be claimed

For any attacker goal whose every route requires an ask-class action, a human decision is forced — regardless of how the attack is phrased, because the classifier never reads content. That guarantee is exactly as strong as the claim that the allow tier is effect-free. It is not. So we publish the residual attack surface instead of a safety claim.
